I develop analytical tools that make complex materials easier to understand—and safer to use.

My research and development experience spans polymer recycling, environmental analysis, spectroscopy, microscopy, renewable fuels, and food chemistry. Across these areas, the goal is consistent: translate careful measurement into solutions that matter to industry, communities, and the environment.

I am equally committed to undergraduate research. The laboratory is a place where students learn to ask precise questions, handle sophisticated instrumentation, and connect fundamental chemistry to challenges beyond the classroom.

Featured project

Making recycled plastics safer.

This NSF-supported work develops a sensitive, rapid method for detecting hazardous volatile organic compounds in recycled plastics using solid-phase microextraction and gas chromatography–mass spectrometry.

The project pairs materials-safety research with hands-on opportunities for Troy University undergraduates to build expertise in state-of-the-art analytical chemistry.

Teaching & mentorship

Preparing students to do science—not simply study it.

My teaching emphasizes sound analytical judgment, thoughtful experimental design, and clear scientific communication. Through coursework and research, students learn how modern chemistry can address challenges in materials, health, industry, and the environment.

Undergraduate researchers gain practical experience with advanced techniques while contributing to work with genuine scientific and public value.
